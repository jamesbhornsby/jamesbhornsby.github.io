The ideation of this project began while I was accessing rare, out of print essay collections on The Internet Archive. I quickly discovered that the workflow used to convert photographed pages into easier to use digitial formats (including flowed text) was ineffective in rendering consistent, favorable results. My thought pattern was that if enough data could be gathered from the page such that the solution not only copied the characters from the page by means of an OCR, but knew what in the image was a page and understood the relationship among the members (or elements) of the page, then it was possible to reliable recronstruct the a given page as a flowed text digitial copy.

When asked at the beginning of the Fall '25 semester whether I would be willing to apply for a position as a CodeCoogs Team Lead and lead through the development a project, I shared my idea to combine deep learning with OCRs and after interviews, I was among those selected to lead.

<br/><img src='/images/mediaDigitizer.png' width='650'>

My team got underway rapidly, and I was fortune to have some real talent working with me. Unfortunately, the we began loosing steam as one collaborator after another was pulled away due the increasing demand of the semester and other projects. I was confident, however that it need few of use to go far with plan I had for the project. Given the complexity of the project, fewer people also meant less time needed to coach designate. However, by the Spring semester, I was the only one still contributing to the project. The majority of team were facing similar shortages, but pressed on we did.

By this time, my team had the majority of the groundwork on the projects biggest hurdle: building a synthetic dataset generator from scratch.
